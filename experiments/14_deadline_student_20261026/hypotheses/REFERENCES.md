# Research basis and limits

1. **Sterman, J. D. (1989).** Modeling Managerial Behavior: Misperceptions of Feedback in a Dynamic Decision Making Experiment. *Management Science*, 35(3), 321-339. [Publisher paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.35.3.321). The study links inventory-system dynamics to feedback misperceptions and estimates an anchoring/adjustment decision model. This motivates separately measuring responses to stock and outstanding commitments. It does not identify a normative behavioral weight from experiment 14's finite-difference assay.

2. **Croson, R., and Donohue, K. (2006).** Behavioral Causes of the Bullwhip Effect and the Observed Value of Inventory Information. *Management Science*, 52(3), 323-336. [Publisher paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0436). Supply-line underweighting and bullwhip persisted despite inventory-information sharing, with some upstream benefits from sharing. Hold information availability constant across conditions and retain role-specific summaries. This paper does not establish the effects of this experiment's deadline or educational text.

3. **Croson, R., Donohue, K., Katok, E., and Sterman, J. (2014).** Order Stability in Supply Chains: Coordination Risk and the Role of Coordination Stock. *Production and Operations Management*, 23(2), 176-196. [Publisher paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1937-5956.2012.01422.x). The experiments show that coordination risk contributes to oscillation/amplification and that extra inventory can buffer its consequences. Our inference is that one model jointly choosing all four roles changes an important mechanism of human Beer Game behavior. Separate contexts improve replication, but cannot restore independent human decision makers.

4. **Sterman, J. D. (2010).** Does Formal System Dynamics Training Improve People's Understanding of Accumulation? *System Dynamics Review*, 26(4), 316-334. [Author manuscript at MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/62f923c7-4d32-448f-baef-85935c3cbb07); [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sdr.447). The study reports improved accumulation understanding following training, with some misunderstandings remaining. It motivates a transfer check; it is not evidence that this short reading must improve Beer Game costs or bullwhip under time pressure in fictional UB students.

These sources establish useful mechanisms and measurement precedents, not guaranteed H1-H4 outcomes. The four sources were checked through publisher or author/institutional pages on 26 October 2026. No real UB student observations are used. The new inference population is repeated generations under the frozen model, prompts and execution conditions.
