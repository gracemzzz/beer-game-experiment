## Verification and interpretation

- Costs are game cost units, not API dollars. Fulfillment is cumulative retailer shipments divided by customer demand, including late deliveries. There is one group per condition, so differences are descriptive.

- Independent checks: all 576 saved orders exactly matched their original API responses; replaying all four games reproduced all saved states and costs. The extreme orders were generated by GPT, not inserted by retry/fallback code.

- A synthetic demand-copying policy in the same simulator scored team cost 156 and 100% cumulative fulfillment. This is a software reference, not an optimal-policy claim or human/GPT evidence; its beta would be unidentifiable.

- Example of the behavior problem: the trained/minimal retailer ordered 1,000,000 at week 14 while demand was 8, backlog 72, inventory 0 and supply line 0. The control/minimal factory ordered 1,000,000 in two successive weeks, including when 1,000,000 was already outstanding.

- Low reasoning averaged 602.2 reasoning tokens per game decision; minimal averaged 0. Median request latency was 4.90 versus 0.916 seconds. Effort separation worked descriptively; it does not establish H2. Zero internal reasoning tokens also does not prove absence of reasoning.

- The four numerically usable beta fits were control/low factory ≈0.500, trained/low factory ≈0.417, trained/low retailer ≈0.000, trained/low wholesaler ≈0.289. Three carry parameter-boundary flags; the retailer fit has R²≈0.105. These are not a sound four-condition group comparison.

- No new paid run or notebook protocol change was launched during this review. The completed pilot remains preserved. GPT-agent results cannot establish the original 60/120-second human time-pressure hypothesis.

## Recommended next steps

1. Do not increase groups_per_cell yet. More runs of this setup will not by themselves solve unidentified beta.

2. Review the explicit 1,000,000-unit bound in the prompt/schema before another model test. Several agents hit it exactly. It may act as an anchor, but this pilot does not prove causation. Remove the prominent arbitrary number or adopt a prospectively justified game limit; do not impose a positive minimum or select orders to obtain significance.

3. Use GPT-5.4 nano as a candidate for a small behavior screen, not an assumed fix. It supports low and medium reasoning; that pair is a new H2_API comparison. First use balanced independent states covering backlog, inventory, and outstanding orders, with a proposed $0.20 spending allowance. Judge stable state sensitivity and decision quality, not a preferred treatment-effect sign.

4. Only if that screen is useful, run one fresh 36-week group per condition, with a proposed $0.50 allowance. The present traffic repriced at GPT-5.4 nano rates is about $0.384 for four groups, but another model/effort may use different tokens and exceed the allowance.

5. Before scaling, require usable beta measurement across the planned groups under predetermined criteria, document exclusions, and choose the sample size from an effect-size/power analysis. Eight groups per condition is the current software reporting floor, not a guarantee of power. If beta remains unidentified, redesign the measurement or explicitly choose supply-chain performance as a different endpoint; do not present performance as a test of the original beta hypotheses.
